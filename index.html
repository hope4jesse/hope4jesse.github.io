Jesse's Journey & Medical Background

Understanding a decade-long fight with Crohn's disease and why surgery is urgently needed now.

Ten years ago, our family began a journey no parent ever expects. Our son, Jesse, was diagnosed with Crohn's disease—a lifelong inflammatory bowel disease that is relatively uncommon and often difficult to diagnose in the Philippines.

What began as a medical condition soon became a decade-long battle marked by uncertainty, pain, repeated hospitalizations, multiple surgeries, and daily physical challenges. Over the past 10 years, our family has spent an estimated ₱5 million to ₱7 million PHP on his medical care, exhausting our savings and resources to give Jesse every chance to heal.

"Although Kuya Jesse and I aren't the closest cousins, I know he is so much more than his illness. He is a kind, strong person with dreams and a future ahead of him. Every share and donation brings him one step closer to getting the surgery he needs."
— Jhian Abrera (Cousin & Organizer)

Today, Jesse faces a critical turning point. He urgently requires a specialized intestinal resection surgery to remove damaged, strictured tissue, prevent life-threatening blockages, and allow his body to properly absorb nutrition again.

Understanding Crohn's Disease & The Philippine Context

Medical facts, global statistics, and the reality of government medical support in the Philippines.

6.8M+ Global Prevalence

Over 6.8 million people worldwide live with Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD).

+200% Surge in Asia-Pacific

IBD incidence in Asia has more than tripled over the past two decades due to dietary and environmental shifts.

75% Lifetime Surgery Rate

Up to 3 in 4 Crohn's patients require surgical resection due to intestinal strictures or blockages.

2–5 Yrs PH Diagnostic Delay

Crohn's is frequently misdiagnosed in the Philippines as Intestinal Tuberculosis due to overlapping symptoms.

🇵🇭 The Reality of Philippine Government Medical Assistance & PhilHealth Coverage

PhilHealth Case Rate Coverage Limits

PhilHealth's standard inpatient benefit package for major intestinal surgeries covers only ₱15,000 to ₱38,000 PHP — leaving over 97% of the ₱1,500,000 total surgical bill to be paid out-of-pocket by the family.

0% Outpatient Biologic Medication Coverage

Biologic infusions (Infliximab / Adalimumab) cost ₱50,000 to ₱120,000 per dose required every 4–8 weeks. PhilHealth currently provides zero reimbursement for outpatient autoimmune biologic therapies in the Philippines.

Malasakit Centers & PCSO Support Gaps

Government programs like Malasakit Centers, DOH MAIFIP, and PCSO offer financial guarantee vouchers for public hospital bills. However, they do not cover specialized private surgical fees, imported ICU total parenteral nutrition (TPN), or outpatient stoma care supplies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions regarding Jesse's health, campaign management, and donation safety.

Why was this dedicated website created?

To centralize split donation pages (GoFundMe & GoGetFunding), eliminate platform transaction fee losses for Philippine supporters who prefer GCash / Maya, and give 100% transparency on surgery progress.

How do I know my donation reaches Jesse directly?

All GCash, Maya, and local bank transfers go directly to Mary Therese Pitogo (Jesse's mother). GoFundMe is managed by Jhian Abrera (cousin in CA, US) and transferred straight to medical billing.

Does PhilHealth or government programs (PCSO / Malasakit) cover Jesse's surgery?

PhilHealth's fixed case rates cover only ₱15,000 to ₱38,000 of a ₱1.5M specialized intestinal resection and ICU hospital admission (under 3% of total costs). PhilHealth also provides 0% coverage for chronic outpatient biologic infusions. While government assistance programs like Malasakit Centers and PCSO offer partial hospital bill guarantees, they cannot cover private surgical specialist fees, ICU total parenteral nutrition (TPN), or ongoing stoma care supplies—making direct community fundraising essential.
